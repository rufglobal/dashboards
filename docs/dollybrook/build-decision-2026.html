02 · The pricing error

June 2026 was sold at 58% of July. It should have been 90%.

The property's own seasonality is stable: June prices at 88-91% of July in both prior years. In 2026 that collapsed. This is not a demand story - June 2026 occupancy was the highest on record. It is a rate that was entered wrong and left running.

03 · Correction

Buyout revenue now follows each cabin's own rate

A whole-resort booking displaces nine specific cabins, each of which would otherwise have sold at its own price. Splitting that revenue by bed count credits Winter Wonderland the same as Dreamy Den and under-credits Captain's Lodge. Splitting it by ADR does not.

Share of a whole-resort booking, by cabin

Property ADR is unaffected - the total is the same, only its distribution across cabins changes. What moves is per-cabin RevPAB, and therefore the capacity-class comparison the build decision rests on.

05 · Unit economics

Bigger cabins earn more per cabin and less per bed

06 · The algorithm

One number decides the cabin size: fixed cost divided by cost per bed

You do not need to know your build costs in dollars to make this decision. You need to know their shape - how much of a cabin's cost is there no matter what size it is, and how much each extra bed adds on top.

Where F is everything a cabin costs regardless of size - site work, foundation, utility runs, permits, one kitchen, one bathroom, design - and v is what each additional bed adds: square footage, framing, finish, and the bathroom-per-two-bedrooms rule of thumb. Only the ratio matters.

The events slider models the common area working: it lifts the share of nights sold as part of a multi-cabin group, which prices at a premium and is size-neutral in bed terms.

Read the break-even directly

If your F/v is above the number in a row, the larger cabin wins that comparison. Below it, the smaller one does.

Why it reduces to a ratio

Revenue per cabin rises with size but not proportionally: a sleeps-6 earns more than a sleeps-4 for 50% more beds, and a sleeps-8 earns more than a sleeps-6 for 33% more beds. Cost rises with size too, but a chunk of it does not.

The bigger that size-independent chunk, the more sense it makes to spread it over more beds. That is the whole argument, and it is why the answer is a ratio and not a size.

08 · The recommendation

Build in three waves, and let the property tell you when

Speed is the part of this decision where the data is least ambiguous. The property is full for roughly six weeks and empty for roughly six months. That argues against one large simultaneous build and in favour of tranches with an explicit trigger between them.
